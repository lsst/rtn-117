\section{The LSST Camera (LSSTCam)} \label{sec:lsstcam}

The Vera C. Rubin Observatory's camera LSSTCam \cite{10.71929/rubin/2571927} is the largest digital camera in the world for astronomy.
Its design, and the technical progress it demanded, were developed in order to meet the requirements set by the ambitious goals of LSST, as described in \cite{2024SPIE13096E..1SR}.

% Focal plane
The LSSTCam focal plane is a mosaic centrally composed of 189 CCDs used for science imaging, organized into 3$\times$3 rafts, in a focal plane mosaic presented in \citet{CTN-001}.
Each raft is composed of three Readout Electronics Boards (REB) providing the power and electronics of three neighboring CCDs in a row.
The corners of the mosaic contain two CCDs for guiding and one split-wavefront CCD offset intra- and extra-focally relative to the science CCDs.

% CCDs
LSSTCam CCDs are read out in 16 channels, each with their own readout electronics and amplifiers.
Each channel is 2048 pixels high and 512 pixels wide for E2V and 2048 pixels high and 576 pixels wide for ITL sensors.
Figure \ref{fig:isr-before-after} shows one E2V and one ITL detector from the same exposure before and after instrument signal removal.
The raw frames retain the serial and parallel overscan, which are virtual pixels read to estimate charge transfer inefficiency (CTI), see \ref{sec:processing:calibration:cti} for more details.

\subsection{Physical Model of Photon Transport in LSSTCam CCDs} \label{sec:lsstcam:detector-model}\label{sec:processing}


Calibration and ISR tasks are organized as a pipeline based on our current model of the detector physics since instrument artifacts are removed in the reverse chronological order in which they compound along the signal chain.

The calibration and instrument signal removal (ISR) therefore needs a clear picture of the signal chain, from the point of conversion of photons in the detector to the serialized digital records, as well as
physical models of each systematic transformation that applies to the signal in each step.

Figure \ref{fig:detector-boxes} depicts the different steps in this chain and their physical ordering from the detector
to the Readout Board (REB). 

The algorithms and processing steps required for LSSTCam calibration and ISR were developed from measurements and analysis during electro-optical testing on the laboratory test stand at SLAC National Accelerator Laboratory, at the summit, and on the telescope in-dome during commissioning.
\citet{2024SPIE13096E..1SR} gives a detailed account of system characterization work and results from the laboratory campaigns.
Figure~\ref{fig:detector-boxes} summarizes the instrumental effects identified in the photon transfer chain and their order.
