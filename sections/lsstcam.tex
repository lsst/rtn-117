\section{The LSST Camera (LSSTCam)} \label{sec:lsstcam}

The design of the LSSTCam \cite{10.71929/rubin/2571927} was developed in order to meet the strict requirements set by the ambitious goals of LSST \citep{2019ApJ...873..111I, RTN-011}, as described in \cite{2024SPIE13096E..1SR}.

% Focal plane
The LSSTCam focal plane is a mosaic centrally composed of 189 CCDs used for science imaging, organized into 3$\times$3 rafts, in a focal plane mosaic presented in \citet{CTN-001}.
Each raft is composed of three Readout Electronics Boards (REB) providing the power and electronics of three neighboring CCDs in a row.
The corners of the mosaic contain two CCDs for guiding and one split-wavefront CCD offset intra- and extra-focally relative to the science CCDs.

% CCDs
LSSTCam CCDs are read out in 16 channels, each with their own readout electronics and signal amplifiers.
Each channel is 2048 pixels high and 512 pixels wide for E2V and 2048 pixels high and 576 pixels wide for ITL sensors.
Charge is shifted down the columns (the \emph{parallel} direction) into a serial register, which carries each row to the amplifier (the \emph{serial} direction).
Each row ends with extra serial overscan columns, and the image ends with extra parallel overscan rows (Table~\ref{tab:amp-geometry}).

\subsection{Physical Model of Photon Transport in LSSTCam CCDs} \label{sec:lsstcam:detector-model}\label{sec:processing}

The physical properties of the detectors and readout electronics imprint instrumental signatures that cause each recorded pixel value to depart from the incident signal.
Our model of the conversion of photons to charge and the subsequent digitization, was established through electro-optical characterization on the laboratory test stand at SLAC National Accelerator Laboratory, at the summit, and on the telescope during commissioning.
\citet{2024SPIE13096E..1SR} gives a detailed account of system characterization work and results from the laboratory campaigns.

Calibration and instrument signature removal (ISR) tasks are organized as a pipeline based on our current model of the detector physics since instrument artifacts are removed in the reverse chronological order in which they compound along the signal chain.
The algorithms therefore need a clear picture of the signal chain, as well as physical models of each systematic transformation that applies to the signal in each step.
Figure \ref{fig:detector-boxes} depicts where artifacts enter in this chain from the detector to the Readout Board (REB).

The chain begins in the silicon, where incident photons are converted to electrons that collect in the pixel potential wells.
Two effects enter here: dark current adds charge that is thermally generated rather than photo-generated, and the brighter-fatter effect redistributes charge to neighboring pixels as the wells fill.
Because both act on the charge before it is moved, they are the first effects in the chain, and the last to be corrected.
The charge packets are then shifted down the columns by parallel transfer and along the serial register toward the amplifier, where a fraction of each packet is left behind as charge transfer inefficiency (CTI) and serial trapping.
This shifting precedes the amplifier, so deferred charge is still present in the image in units of electrons.
At the output node, the on-chip amplifier converts charge to voltage with gain $g_1$, and from this point the signal is carried in volts rather than electrons.
The signal then passes through the cabling to the REB, where crosstalk couples signals between neighboring amplifiers and across the midline, after which the ASPIC applies a second, temperature-dependent gain ($g_2$).
Non-linearities at high, mid, and low signal levels enter at different points along this analog path, and the dual-slope integrator, with a third gain $g_3$, forms the voltage difference between the reset and signal levels.
Only after this analog processing is a clock-injected offset added by the bias injection stage, which produces the 2D bias structure and the bias ``wave'', and the signal is finally digitized by the ADC, whose differential non-linearity (DNL) is the last effect imposed.
The overall gain $g$ describes the net conversion from electrons to digital ADU.
